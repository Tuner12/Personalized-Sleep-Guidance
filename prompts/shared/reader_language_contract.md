# Reader-Language Contract v1

Before returning the recommendation, make the four Sections sound like clear advice written
for one person. Use plain, fluent professional English and familiar words. Preserve every
fact, number, comparison, uncertainty, condition, Evidence boundary, and action.

- Give each sentence one main observation, comparison, interpretation, or action.
- Split a sentence when it contains three or more metrics moving in different directions.
- Let sentence order carry the logic when possible. Use a connector only when it adds a
  relationship the reader needs.
- Avoid compressed or formulaic links such as `even as` and `therefore`. Use `while`,
  `although`, or `but` only for one clear contrast, never several contrasts in one sentence.
- Prefer a person-centered verb when it is more natural than an abstract metric trend. Write
  `you spent less time awake after falling asleep`, not `time awake after falling asleep
  decreased`. Write `you slept longer`, not `sleep duration increased`, when both preserve the
  same precise meaning.
- Use pronouns naturally when they have one clear nearby antecedent. Repeat the specific noun
  only when a pronoun could refer to more than one metric, period, condition, action, or
  outcome.
- Make conditions explicit without forcing a template. The condition may come before or after
  the action and may use wording such as `if`, `when`, or `only when`.
- Prefer direct verbs over nominalized explanations. State what changed and what to do instead
  of saying that a pattern `would be a reason to` take an action.
- State feedback directly: `If sleep lasts longer, the added time is helping.` Avoid indirect
  verdicts such as `A repeated increase would show or indicate...`.
- Describe an unusual night plainly. Avoid audit-style phrases such as `a clear departure from
  the pattern`, `worth addressing`, or calling an observed change `an important contributor`.
- Do not expose research, Evidence, retrieval, audit, device, or statistical process language.
- Do not improve flow by inventing a cause, symptom, preference, target, treatment, or user
  context.
- When an action comes from an observed association rather than a causal finding, Follow-up
  may say that the same pattern continues. Do not say the action `is helping` or caused the
  outcome.

Keep Summary longitudinal and selective, Pattern Analysis interpretive, Personalized Recommendation personal and
actionable, and Follow-up concise. Follow-up must connect the repeated outcome to a clear
decision about whether to keep, adjust, or stop the specific action, or when to seek the
selected clinical discussion. Remove repetition across Sections before returning the
structured object.
Write a seven-hour sleep-duration comparison as `7 hours` or `7 hours or more`, never as
`420 minutes`; retain minutes for shorter durations and time-awake measures when appropriate.
Keep the comparison grammatical: write `below 7 hours` or `well below 7 hours`, never
`below 7 hours or more`. If less time in bed limits sleep duration, say that directly rather
than using `little opportunity for longer sleep`.
